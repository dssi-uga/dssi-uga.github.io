## Overview

This topic studies additive manufacturing as an interconnected multi-stage system rather than a sequence of isolated process steps. In direct ink writing, decisions made during ink preparation, printing, drying, and product characterization jointly determine printability, structural integrity, energy efficiency, geometry retention, cracking behavior, and final material performance. Our work develops system-level learning frameworks that connect these stages through shared process and material information. One example is MMO-Net, a multi-stage modeling and multi-criteria optimization framework that uses cross-stage feature transition and multi-task prediction to model rheology, printing time, geometric fidelity before and after drying, and cracking probability. By coupling predictive modeling with Bayesian optimization, this research helps identify process settings that balance competing objectives and reduce trial-and-error experimentation in advanced additive manufacturing.
