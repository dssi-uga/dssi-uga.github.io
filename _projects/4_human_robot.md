## Overview

This topic focuses on data-driven monitoring and prediction for carbon-negative biomass material manufacturing. Ambient drying is a promising low-cost and scalable process because it reduces energy use and can preserve structural integrity, but the process is difficult to control because drying kinetics vary with material formulation, airflow, humidity, and sample-level heterogeneity. Our work uses real-time gravimetric sensing to observe dynamic weight changes during drying and builds hierarchical nonlinear mixed-effects models to capture both population-level drying behavior and sample-specific variation. By incorporating material composition, fan speed, and Bayesian updating from live measurements, the framework improves prediction during an ongoing drying process and provides interpretable insight into how composition and airflow shape initial weight, drying rate, moisture retention, and future control decisions.
